#### How climate-related geodata can support sustainable tourism planning and development

## Use Case 1 – Climate Changes
The **Climate Change Explorer for Tourism (CCET)** is a joint initiative by GeoVille, Österreich Werbung, EOX, and BRZ within Digital Twin Austria (GTIF-AT EA). It provides Austria's tourism regions with a user-friendly analysis of climate change impacts and risks. This section explores the first of three key use cases featured in the tool.

### What it's about
This use case visualizes projected climate changes using metrics directly relevant to the tourism sector. It serves as the foundation of the tool, addressing a critical question: *How is the climate in a specific region changing, and what long-term trends can be derived from it?*

### The data behind it
The indicators are based on the **ÖKS15 climate projection dataset** from GeoSphere Austria [1]. These projections are calculated under two IPCC scenarios:
*   **RCP4.5**: A moderate, optimistic pathway.
*   **RCP8.5**: A pessimistic, high-emissions pathway.

Data covers temperature, precipitation, and wind at a spatial resolution of 1 km, on a daily basis, spanning from 2011 to 2100. Grid cell values are aggregated for municipalities, elevation zones, and tourism destination boundaries to provide actionable climatological insights.

## Climatic Indicators
The CCET measures several key indicators to assess climate impacts:

*   **Thermal Comfort:** Hot days (>30°C), Tropical nights (>20°C), and Beach/lake days (>23°C + solar radiation).
*   **Winter & Extreme Events:** Ice days (<0°C), Heat wave duration index (HWDI), and Strong precipitation days (≥10 mm).
*   **Operational Conditions:** Dry days (<1 mm) and Strong wind days (>11.1 m/s).

### Benefit for users
*   **Strategic Planning:** Regions can compare destinations to build long-term sustainable tourism strategies.
*   **Local Adaptation:** Individual destinations receive clear guidance for climate-friendly infrastructure development.
*   **Scenario Comparison:** By weighing moderate vs. high-emission futures up to 2100, planners can prepare for climate shifts well in advance.

[1] https://data.hub.geosphere.at/group/oks15
